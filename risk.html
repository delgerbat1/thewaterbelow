How the screen works

Each district is tiered on three things. A shortage then gets priced by what households must buy to stay above basic need.

Hazard · city-wide

How short the supply falls

Satellite gravity data (GRACE) can’t tell districts apart: all of Ulaanbaatar sits inside one ~300 km cell [D01]. So the hazard is a single shortfall scenario for the whole city, checked against published Tuul modelling [D09] [D10].

Exposure · by district

Who a shortfall reaches

The number of people multiplied by the share of their supply that comes from the central Tuul wellfields [D05] [D15]. Nearly all city water is groundwater [D14].

Vulnerability · by district

How close to the edge they live

The share of people on kiosk or other non-piped supply. Starting from 17 L, there is no slack: lost water must be bought back at a higher price, or done without.

cut = shortfall × wellfield share
replaced (L/day) = min( cut × use , max(0, B − use × (1 − cut)) )
extra cost per person per year = 365 × replaced / 1000 × (replacement price − usual price)
cut, not bought (L/day) = cut × use − replaced expected annual cost = scenario cost × annual probability

B is the basic-need line (default 20 L, WHO [D12]). Above B, households are assumed to cut use rather than buy. That cut is reported in litres, not money, because we have no sourced value for it. For piped households it is mostly discretionary use; for kiosk users it is water they go without. Prices are per m³.

Data catalog

Every dataset the screen uses or will use: what it measures, its resolution, how to get it, and what it cannot support. Well locations are confidential in Mongolia, so we work only with aggregated or anonymized data and never scrape or guess well points [D08].

Methods

The full method will be a six-page PDF. This is its outline and the rules it will follow.

What this screen deliberately does not do

  • It does not estimate groundwater by district. GRACE’s footprint is about 300 km, and well data are confidential.
  • It does not forecast. Shortfall scenarios are labelled assumptions; the GRACE trend and published models only bound them.
  • It does not give a welfare value for water people go without. That loss is reported in litres.
  • It treats dwelling type as a proxy for connection type. These are not the same, and the gap will be stated.
  • It is not engineering-grade. Use it to ask better questions, not to size infrastructure.

Six-page methods PDF: outline

  1. 1 · Question and scopeWho bears the cost of a groundwater shortfall in Ulaanbaatar; a screening-level framing; what “cost” includes and excludes.
  2. 2 · HazardGRACE/GRACE-FO storage minus GLDAS soil moisture and snow; trend with uncertainty; comparison with Nakayama et al. (2021) and Dechinlkhundev & Zorigt (2021); how scenarios are set.
  3. 3 · Exposure and vulnerabilityCensus population and dwelling type by district; wellfield service shares; the proxy problem.
  4. 4 · Cost ruleThe basic-need replacement rule; price sources; scenario versus expected cost; worked example for one district.
  5. 5 · Results and sensitivityTiers; the share of people versus the share of cost; which assumptions move the answer most.
  6. 6 · Limits, review and data catalogWhat would make it engineering-grade; reviewer comments; a pointer to the catalog and repository.
